> *Vespera* — the evening. Not the night, which has not come; not the dusk, which is
> approaching. The hour when the light is still good and the shadows have begun to lengthen.

---

## How to read it

**The inhabitants are not reliable narrators, and the setting is built upon that.**

The chronicles of the Old World were written by men who had inherited a vocabulary older
than their understanding of it. When a chronicle says *demon*, it means a thing out of the
Astral for which no human tongue has a word. When it says *elf*, it means a people who were
ancient when the first human kingdom was raised, and who would not recognise the name. When
it says *dragon*, it may mean almost anything, and the dwarves who kept the better records
were careful to write *either a dragon or a demon* and leave it there.

None of this is deception. It is what happens when a civilisation survives its own
scholarship. Nearly everything the Old World believes about itself is half true, and the
false half is generally the more interesting.

## The Old World

The setting is one continent, called **Endaran** by its elder inhabitants and **the Old
World** by everyone else. Across the western ocean lie the **Westerlands**, known to the
eastern kingdoms chiefly by rumour and by the occasional ship. Southward, beyond the Kara
River, stand the troll empires of **Gantumba**, which have twice come north in force.
Between the two runs the **Seren**: a desert now almost impassable, which was open savannah
eight thousand years ago, and which several nations have a story to explain.

It is a world living in the aftermath of an empire. **Sarcera** ruled from the hills of
Berland to the Daracian Sea for more than a thousand years, and has been gone for fourteen
hundred. In its place stands a patchwork of successor realms — **Thetland**, **Ascania**,
**Ancora**, **Darde**, **Berland**, **Lythe**, **Azade** and the **Eastern Kingdoms** —
held loosely together by the **Council of Kings** and the **Church of
the Eternal Sun**, and divided by very nearly everything else.

Two monuments dominate the map. A wall runs sea to sea across the south, raised by a
Sarceran emperor who had watched his predecessor spend twenty years and a good share of the
treasury trying to settle the southern question by arms. He concluded that the forests and
swamps beyond were not worth what they would cost to hold, and that a border kept was the
cheaper article. In the narrow sense he was right. What nobody weighed was that the
provinces beyond the wall had been the empire's gateway to the wider world and its chief
source of invention, and that an empire which shuts its own door begins, quite quietly, to
stagnate.

The second monument is **Haldorak**, the greatest city the dwarves ever built, which closed
its gates two thousand years ago without explanation and has sent no word since.

The common tongue is **Thetch**, spread by merchants rather than by armies.

## The received chronology

What follows is the reckoning held by the learned of the Old World, and taught in its
schools. Like every such chronology it grows more confident as it approaches the present,
and more generous with its numbers as it recedes from it; the two elder ages are not
history, and were never meant to be taken as history. Both are nonetheless the memory of
something, kept for forty thousand years by people who had no means of understanding what
they were keeping.

| Age | As reckoned | What is held to have happened |
|---|---|---|
| **The Age of Gods** | from about 40,000 years ago | Divine powers descend upon a world then called **Rhun** and set it in order, appointing seven guardians — associated with the Sun, the Moon, **Corcas**, **Sothos**, **Romon**, **Eracas** and **Larcan**. Over ten thousand years the guardians shape the races of the world. The seventh and brightest, delving into what lies beyond the heavens in order to arm himself against it, is warped by the study into the thing he feared. The age ends in a war that breaks the world in two and sinks its central lands for ever. He is imprisoned; his servants, the **Demon Lords**, are not. |
| **The Age of Myth** | ~22,000 to ~11,000 years ago | The sorcerer-king **Anu** opens a gate out of the shattered world. The Demon Lord **Marach** raises an empire over the ruins of the south and east, and is at last thrown down — by a hero, it is said, though learned men have disputed it for three thousand years, not least because his influence has never wholly gone. The first kingdom of men and the first of the dwarves, **Dhur'ad**, rise and fall together in his war. The greater part of what is now told as legend is set in this age. |
| **The Age of Mortals** | ~11,000 years ago to the present | Ordinary history, in four parts and a fifth still running. |
| *— The Aftermath* | ~11,000 to ~7,000 | The scattering. The first dwarven city that can be called historical, **Halbrad**, rises and falls to something the chronicles decline to name, and the dwarves withdraw north to build **Haldorak**. The **Seren** ceases to be grassland. |
| *— The Dawn of Man* | ~7,000 to ~3,000 | The age of migrations. The **Anân** come down from the northern forests to the Caphenthes Plain and raise **Ûrdâg** upon the Ethoras; the **Talud** arrive after them, take their cities, gods and customs, and replace their language entirely. Ûrdâg becomes Raphon, and the Anân are forgotten within a few centuries of having invented nearly everything. |
| *— The Classical Age* | ~3,000 to ~1,400 | **Sarcera**: a thousand years of empire, ending in a long stagnation of its own making. The **War of the Waning Moon** brings the goatmen out of the south. The **Church of the Eternal Sun** is made state doctrine under an emperor whose greatness proves to be the death-throes of the thing he restored. |
| *— The Middle Ages* | ~1,400 to ~300 | Gothic tribes take the imperial heartland and claim the inheritance. The trolls of Gantumba launch two **Great Conquests**, the second reaching the edge of the far east before it dies in the desert. The **Council of Kings** is formed. |
| *— Recent history* | ~300 years ago to now | The **War of Six Crowns** settles the shape of the modern north, binds the last independent kingdoms into the Council, and makes **Berland** a naval power to rival Ancora. By tradition it is this war, and not any event of statecraft, that divides the middle age from the present one. |

The division itself is worth noticing. A chronology of successive ages before the present
one — each longer, grander and less documented than the last, ending in a catastrophe that
clears the ground for the next — is the shape every learned tradition of the Old World
arrives at, whether it counts its ages in gods, in metals or in kings. That the traditions
agree is taken by the Church as proof. It is taken by the dwarves as evidence of nothing
more than a common source, which they consider a different matter entirely.

## The peoples

The names men give their neighbours are rarely the names those neighbours use.

- **Men**, in a great many nations, across both continents. Their own origin is unknown to
  them, though the physical likeness between the races suggests one.
- **Dwarves**, out of Haldorak. They keep *Adranal* — chronicles — and are in consequence
  the most reliable historians in the world. They live two hundred years, and some of them
  sleep through the winter.
- **The Taldar**, called **Hulden** in the common tongue and *elves* by almost everyone.
  Taller and fairer than men, with short tufted tails and prominent canines, they keep to
  the forests where forests remain and hide what marks them out where they do not.
- **Gnomes**, the smallest of the mortal races at a little over two feet, subterranean,
  ubiquitous, and generally regarded as vermin. The names *goblin*, *kobold* and *hob* are
  all insults for the same people.
- **The Hadur**, the goatmen. They were men once, and were made into this; what remains of
  that knowledge is held mostly by them.
- **Trolls**, so called, who hold the whole of Gantumba under the **Fakal**, and who have a
  classical civilisation of their own that the north has never troubled to study.
- **The Taer'zal** of the Westerlands, four-legged and maned, who serve as their own cavalry.
- And the **Kar'aqa**, reclusive and singular; the **Necks** of the coasts and rivers; and
  the last **giants**, in the far north.

## Magic

Magic in Vespera is not a discipline of spells but a property of the world. The material
universe is permeated by **aether**, and souls are a dense and unstable concentration of it;
to work magic is to reach the incorporeal and reshape the corporeal through it.

Aether obeys something much like entropy. A soul without a vessel disperses into the astral
sea, which means a world cannot produce complex souls at all unless something holds them
together long enough. This world has such a thing. It is asleep beneath the ground, it has
been there longer than any record, and a good deal of what the Old World calls the spirit
realm is its dream.
